Letter of engagement

Meridian Harbor Logistics requests an assessment of the technology supporting our dispatch, customer shipment tracking, finance reporting, and recently acquired warehouse operation. Our next major customer requires assurance that customer records and internal business systems are appropriately protected.

Please assess the environment as a connected business. We need clear evidence of business impact, practical recommendations, and a prioritized remediation plan that a small IT team can carry out. A list of scanner findings alone will not meet our objectives.

Company context

We coordinate regional freight and temperature-controlled deliveries. Dispatch staff rely on Windows domain accounts and shared documents; customers use a Linux-hosted shipment portal. Warehouse deployment and backup tooling was retained after an acquisition because replacing it would disrupt operations. Integration was performed under a tight schedule and documentation is uneven.

Rules of engagement

  • Assess only the assigned targets and supplied application URLs/accounts.
  • Use controlled proofs of concept and synthetic records to demonstrate impact; explain the attack conditions and resulting business risk.
  • Preserve service availability and evidence. Coordinate before disruptive changes, account lockouts, bulk data changes, or persistence testing.
  • Stop and report access to an unexpected environment or another team's data.
  • Exclude denial of service, destructive encryption, uncontrolled malware, social engineering of real people, and attacks on public Internet systems.
  • Hypervisors, storage infrastructure, the range controller, Guacamole, shared firewall, underlying management networks and other teams are out of scope. Do not attempt to escape a guest or compromise these systems.

Requested deliverables

  1. An executive summary describing material business risks.
  2. A technical report with affected assets, reproducible evidence, realistic impact, severity rationale, and remediation.
  3. A connected attack narrative where findings combine into greater impact.
  4. A prioritized remediation roadmap, separating immediate containment from longer-term improvements.
  5. A short technical handoff and executive briefing.

Use screenshots and minimal proof-of-concept artifacts where useful. Keep credentials out of general report copies and submit sensitive material through the organizer's designated channel. Include assumptions, limitations, and unverified findings explicitly.
